# Genre: Science Fiction

## reader promise
The "what if": a rigorously imagined novum (one new idea — a technology, a social
order, a scientific premise) followed honestly to its human consequences. Readers come
for ideas that illuminate something about us, and for a world extrapolated with
internal logic. The promise is wonder earned by consistency and consequence.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- Early, clear establishment of the central novum and the world's altered rules.
- Consequences of the idea dramatised through character, not lectured.
- A protagonist whose problem is entangled with the novum.
- Escalation that tests the idea's implications (cost, ethics, second-order effects).
- A climax where the idea's logic pays off (the resolution flows from the premise,
  not despite it).

## what successful stories do
- comps by mode: hard SF (Andy Weir, *Project Hail Mary*; Ted Chiang), social/literary
  (Le Guin, *The Left Hand of Darkness*; Ishiguro, *Klara and the Sun*), space opera
  (Leckie, *Ancillary Justice*), dystopia (Atwood). Match the mode to the premise.
- They commit to ONE big idea and follow it rigorously rather than scattering gadgets.
- Exposition is rationed and motivated; the reader learns the world by living in it.
- The technology/idea is a lens on a human/ethical question (theme).

## tropes to honour
- first contact, the AI awakening, the generation ship, time travel paradox, the
  dystopian regime, terraforming, the uplift. Each carries reader expectations.

## cliches to avoid
- info-dump exposition / technobabble that explains instead of dramatises;
- a novum with inconsistent rules (breaks the logic contract);
- the idea introduced but never followed to consequences;
- humans-are-special / aliens-as-rubber-foreheads with no real otherness;
- science so hand-wavy it's fantasy wearing a lab coat (unless that's the intent).

## structure note
Three-act works; the climax must resolve via the premise's own logic. Keep a bible of
the world's rules and tech so consequences stay consistent across the book.

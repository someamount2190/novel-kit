# Genre: Romance

## reader promise
Emotional catharsis and a GUARANTEED emotionally satisfying ending — the HEA (happily
ever after) or HFN (happy for now). Readers come for the longing, the push-pull, and
the earned union of two people who change each other. Breaking the happy-ending
promise breaks the genre contract; it becomes a love story, not a romance.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- The meet (or re-meet) that establishes the spark and the friction.
- A clear, believable BARRIER keeping them apart (internal wound + external obstacle).
- Rising intimacy beats — the moments that bond them, alternating with conflict.
- The "dark moment" / black moment: the barrier wins, they break apart, all seems lost.
- The grand gesture / reckoning where one (or both) changes and risks vulnerability.
- The HEA/HFN: the union, earned by visible internal change in both.

## what successful stories do
- comps by sub-genre: contemporary (Emily Henry), historical (Tessa Dare, Bridgerton),
  romantasy (Sarah J. Maas). Match heat level and sub-genre to the premise.
- They give BOTH leads a want/need and an arc; the romance forces both to grow.
- The barrier is real and character-driven, not a misunderstanding a conversation
  would fix.
- They escalate intimacy and stakes in tandem; each beat raises what's at risk.
- Banter / voice differentiation makes the leads feel like distinct, specific people.

## tropes to honour
- enemies-to-lovers, friends-to-lovers, second-chance, forced proximity, fake
  relationship, grumpy/sunshine, slow burn. Tropes are PROMISES — deliver the beats
  readers chose the trope for.

## cliches to avoid
- the "big misunderstanding" barrier solvable by one honest sentence;
- a love interest who is perfect (no flaw, no arc);
- insta-love with no bonding beats;
- a HEA that isn't earned by internal change;
- the third-act break-up that exists only to pad length.

## structure note
The romance beat sheet (Gwen Hayes, *Romancing the Beat*) or three-act. The climax is
the EMOTIONAL reckoning + reunion; the tension curve peaks at the black moment then
resolves. Track BOTH characters' arcs as subplots.

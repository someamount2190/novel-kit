# Genre: Isekai (portal fantasy, summoned/transported hero)

> Researched live 2026-06-21 (SOP-10) across CBR, GameRant, ScreenRant, Ranker,
> ComicBook, Royal Road & Scribble Hub forums, Quora, HubPages — cross-checked ≥2
> sources per claim. Sub-genre of portal fantasy (Alice/Oz lineage) in the Japanese
> light-novel tradition. The premise here is the WEAK/ABANDONED-hero variant.

## reader promise
Escapist wish-fulfilment: a glimpse of a world more exciting than the real one, and
the fantasy of being transported out of an ordinary/powerless life into one where you
matter — agency, recognition, a second chance. The genre grew from hikikomori-culture
escapism, so the emotional core is "what if I started over somewhere my life counted."
The WEAK-HERO variant promises a sharper pleasure: the underdog underestimated and
then proven — survival and worth earned by wit, grit, and allies rather than handed
over as raw power.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- The CROSSING — the summoning/transport into the other world (the portal beat).
- The new world's RULES established, usually as a game-like system (stats, classes,
  skills, levels).
- The hero's ROLE/STATUS conferred — summoned by a kingdom/king to face a threat
  (demon king, calamity, monsters).
- The APPRAISAL — the hero's power/class is read/measured. This is the genre's pivot
  scene; in the weak-hero variant it's where he's found wanting and devalued.
- ADAPTATION — learning the world's logic, language, dangers (the weak-hero variant
  lingers here: the brutal reality of being unprepared).
- The hero finds an UNCONVENTIONAL value/path (defense/strategy, a "useless" skill,
  knowledge) that the system undervalued.
- A QUEST/THREAT that tests the earned competence; growth paid for in struggle.

## what successful stories do
- comps (weak/abandoned-hero variant): *The Rising of the Shield Hero* (summoned,
  scorned, wins by defense + strategy + allies), *Grimgar: Ashes and Illusions*
  (powerless, brutal survival), *Re:Zero* (one bleak "Return by Death", not strength),
  *Ascendance of a Bookworm* (frail, value via knowledge/books), *Handyman Saitō*
  (ordinary skills become the party's backbone). Study the precise sub-genre.
- They EARN the glow-up — they dramatise the socially-stunted/weak protagonist's
  struggle into competence rather than skipping to noble hero.
- They give the powerlessness real stakes and let the hero find a strength the
  system/society overlooked (the theme is usually "worth isn't what the system measures").
- Allies are real people with their own wants, not ego-props.
- The game-system/appraisal is a meaningful GATE (a verdict with consequences), not a
  numbers readout.

## tropes to honour
- the summoning circle; the status/appraisal screen; the demon-king threat; the
  party; the kingdom's politics; the "unique/trash skill" that's secretly powerful;
  the modern-knowledge edge (use sparingly + cleverly).

## cliches to avoid
- the OVERPOWERED, never-threatened protagonist (if he's always strongest, there's no
  story — the universe bending to save him is the #1 reader complaint);
- the UNEARNED glow-up (loser → noble hero with no shown struggle);
- the HAREM of hollow female love interests who exist to prop the MC's ego; women whose
  only trait is loving him;
- ROMANTICISED SLAVERY (the "buy a slave girl" beat) — a major reader offender;
- gratuitous FANSERVICE that overshadows plot/character;
- modern-knowledge-solves-everything deployed thoughtlessly;
- trope-by-numbers world indistinguishable from a hundred others.

## structure note
Hero's Journey / three-act fits, with the APPRAISAL as the Act-I turn (here: judged
powerless, discarded). For the weak-hero variant the tension curve must rise on EARNED
competence + escalating threat — never grant a power that removes danger. Keep a strict
bible for the world's system rules + names. The strongest versions are also a quiet
CRITIQUE of the power-fantasy genre: the hero nobody wanted proves worth the genre's
usual metric can't see.

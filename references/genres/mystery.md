# Genre: Mystery

## reader promise
The pleasure of a puzzle that plays fair: a crime disrupts order, and the reader is
invited to solve it alongside (or ahead of) the detective, then rewarded with a
solution that was always reachable from the clues. Underneath the puzzle, the deeper
promise is the restoration of justice/order and the satisfaction of hidden truth
dragged into the light.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- The crime (or its discovery) early — usually a body / a disappearance.
- A detective figure with a method, given a reason to care.
- Clues planted FAIRLY and in view of the reader (fair-play); red herrings that
  mislead honestly, not by withholding.
- Interrogations / investigation scenes that both reveal and complicate.
- A midpoint reversal — the obvious suspect clears, or the case deepens.
- The reveal/confrontation: the detective names the culprit and shows the chain of
  reasoning; the reader can look back and see it was earned.
- Consequences / restored order (or pointed disorder in noir).

## what successful stories do
- comps span cosy (Christie) to procedural (Connelly) to literary (Tana French,
  *In the Woods*; *The Dry*, Jane Harper). Study the sub-genre the premise implies.
- They make the detective's PERSONAL stake mirror the case's theme.
- They control information ruthlessly: the reader always knows what the detective
  knows (fair-play) but not what they INFER — the gap is the suspense.
- The setting is a character (the town, the institution) and the source of motive.
- The culprit is present early and plausible in hindsight.

## tropes to honour
- the least-likely-suspect; the closed circle of suspects; the ticking second crime;
  the detective's foil/sidekick; the final-act gathering.

## cliches to avoid
- the solution turning on info withheld from the reader (cheating);
- a brand-new character introduced as the killer in the last chapter;
- the detective's leap with no shown reasoning ("a hunch");
- the villain's monologue that explains everything the plot failed to dramatise;
- "it was all a dream" / twin-nobody-mentioned.

## structure note
Three-act or the clue-by-clue escalation both work; the climax is the REVEAL, so
the tension curve peaks at the confrontation, not a chase. Keep a setup/payoff ledger
of every clue.

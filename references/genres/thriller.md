# Genre: Thriller

## reader promise
Relentless forward pull and escalating dread: a competent protagonist faces a
powerful, active threat under time pressure, and the reader rides the adrenaline of
"what happens next" with the stakes rising each turn. The promise is tension that
rarely slackens and a payoff equal to the threat.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- An early, concrete threat with a deadline or a clock (race against time).
- A protagonist competent enough to plausibly fight back, but outmatched.
- A powerful, ACTIVE antagonist with agency and a plan (not a passive mystery).
- Set-piece scenes of escalating danger; each "win" raises the cost.
- A midpoint that widens the threat (it's bigger/closer than thought).
- A lowest point where the plan fails and the protagonist is cornered.
- A climax where the protagonist, by competence and will, turns the tables.

## what successful stories do
- comps: Harlan Coben, Gillian Flynn (*Gone Girl* — domestic), Lee Child (action),
  *The Silent Patient*. Match the sub-genre (psychological / spy / domestic / techno).
- They cut scenes late and leave early; chapters end on hooks/reversals.
- They keep the antagonist a step ahead so the hero reacts, then flips it at the end.
- Stakes are personal AND external (save the world / save the marriage AND survive).
- They withhold and time reveals for maximum reader anxiety, not confusion.

## tropes to honour
- the ticking clock; the betrayal by a trusted ally; the false victory; the
  cat-and-mouse; the protagonist with a special skill and a personal wound.

## cliches to avoid
- the villain who pauses to explain instead of acting;
- the idiot-plot (danger only because everyone behaves stupidly);
- cavalry-out-of-nowhere resolution (the hero must drive the climax);
- a competent hero who suddenly forgets their competence for plot convenience;
- torture-porn / shock with no stakes behind it.

## structure note
Save the Cat or three-act with a hard midpoint escalation. The tension curve should
climb almost monotonically; flat stretches are fatal — every scene raises or tightens.

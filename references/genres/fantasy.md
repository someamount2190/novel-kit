# Genre: Fantasy

## reader promise
Immersion in a coherent secondary world with its own rules, and a sense of wonder —
magic, scope, the numinous — paired with a quest or struggle whose stakes feel
mythic. Readers come for a world they can live in and a hero's journey that earns its
awe. The promise is wonder grounded by consistent rules.

## conventions / obligatory scenes
- Early establishment of the world's RULES (esp. how magic works + its cost/limits).
- A call to adventure / disruption that pulls the protagonist from the ordinary.
- Worldbuilding revealed through action and need, not info-dump.
- Escalating trials that test and change the hero; allies and mentors gained/lost.
- A confrontation with the antagonistic force at mythic scale.
- A cost paid — magic, victory, and change should not be free.

## what successful stories do
- comps by scale: epic (Sanderson — hard magic with rules; Jordan), grimdark
  (Abercrombie), character-driven (Le Guin, *Earthsea*), romantasy (Maas). Match scope.
- They make magic a system with COSTS and LIMITS so it can't trivially solve problems
  (Sanderson's Laws: a magic's problem-solving power scales with how well the reader
  understands its rules).
- They reveal the world through a character who has reason to encounter it.
- Theme rides under the spectacle; the quest externalises an internal change.

## tropes to honour
- the chosen one, the mentor, the fellowship, the dark lord, the prophecy, the
  hidden heir, the magic school. Honour or subvert deliberately — don't deploy by default.

## cliches to avoid
- magic with no cost/limit (deus ex machina on tap);
- the prophecy that removes all doubt about the outcome;
- info-dump prologues / "as you know" worldbuilding;
- the evil-for-evil's-sake dark lord with no logic;
- medieval-Europe-by-default with no fresh culture.

## structure note
Hero's Journey or three-act at scale; multi-POV epics still need each thread to
escalate. Maintain a strict bible for world rules + names (continuity over length is
brutal here).

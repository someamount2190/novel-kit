# Structure templates (pick one; fill the beats in the plan)

Templates are scaffolds, not cages — but a first novel with no spine sags. Choose
the one that fits the story's engine and write the beats *with their turns* into the
plan's STRUCTURE section, then expand into the SCENE LEDGER. `structure.py` checks
the resulting tension curve, not which template you used.

## Three-act (default, genre-agnostic)
- **Act I (≈25%)** — ordinary world; INCITING INCIDENT; the protagonist commits at
  the **first plot point** (the door closes behind them).
- **Act II (≈50%)** — rising complications; **midpoint** reversal/revelation that
  recontextualises the goal; things tighten to the **second plot point** (all is
  lost / the false victory or defeat).
- **Act III (≈25%)** — the protagonist, changed, drives to the **climax**; resolution.
- Use when: you want a reliable rising line. The sagging middle lives in Act II —
  give every Act II scene a turn that raises tension.

## Save the Cat (commercial / plot-forward)
15 beats: Opening Image · Theme Stated · Setup · Catalyst · Debate · Break into Two ·
B Story · Fun and Games · Midpoint · Bad Guys Close In · All Is Lost · Dark Night of
the Soul · Break into Three · Finale · Final Image. Use when: tight pacing and a
clear external plot matter (thriller, rom-com, adventure).

## Story Circle (character-forward, Harmon's 8 steps)
You (comfort) → Need → Go (cross the threshold) → Search (adapt) → Find (get it) →
Take (pay the price) → Return (changed) → Change. Use when: the engine is internal
transformation more than external plot (literary, coming-of-age).

## Choosing
- External goal + ticking clock → Three-act or Save the Cat.
- Internal change is the real story → Story Circle.
- Whatever you pick, the SCENE LEDGER's tension must rise across acts and peak late
  (`structure.py`). The template names the beats; the ledger proves the escalation.

# Craft: significant places are characters with history and arc

**Principle.** A place that matters to the story is not a backdrop. It has HISTORY
(what happened there), MEANING (what it represents to characters), and an ARC
(how it changes across the story). The ruined temple isn't just "a temple with
broken columns" — it's where the protagonist's father died, where they first
understood the cost of power, and by story's end it might be reclaimed or
destroyed. Places that earn this treatment become resonant; places that stay
backdrops are forgotten. (Sources: McKee, *Story*; Monaco, *How to Read Literature*
Like a Professor; King, *On Writing* — setting as character.)

**Why the model needs it.** Left to autopilot, the model treats places as generic
containers. A tavern is "a tavern with wooden tables and loud patrons." A castle
is "a castle with stone walls and guards." The prose names features but never
connects them to character memory or story meaning. The result is a white-room
stage set, not a lived-in world. `texture.py` checks world-term density, but it
can't judge whether a place has character significance — that requires design
commitment. The bible must capture each significant place's history and meaning,
just as it captures character dossiers.

**Before (generic backdrop — no history/meaning).**
> They met at the Rusty Anchor. It was a tavern by the sea. Wooden tables.
> Sailors drank there. Mara sat at a table in the corner. The server brought
> ale. They talked about the quest. They left.

The tavern is interchangeable with any other. No character has a relationship
to it. It won't be remembered.

**After (place with history/meaning/arc).**
> They met at the Rusty Anchor — the tavern where Mara's mother used to sing
> ballads, before the sea took her. The same scarred table where Mara had sat
> as a child, listening, still bore the burn mark from the night a drunken
> sailor overturned a lantern. The server, old Joss, remembered Mara: "Little
> songbird's all grown." They talked about the quest, and Mara found herself
> glancing at the empty stage where her mother's portrait still hung. When
> they left, she trailed her hand along the wall — tracing the carved initials
> her father had made the night they met.

The tavern is SPECIFIC. It has history (mother's performances, father's initials),
meaning (loss, legacy), and character relationships (Joss, the memory). The place
changes Mara by being there. It will be remembered.

**Discriminating check.** For any place the POV character visits more than once,
ask: (1) What HAPPENED here before? (2) What does this place MEAN to the character?
(3) How does the place CHANGE across the story? If you can't answer all three,
the place is a backdrop — either give it history/meaning or merge it with a
place that has. A place without history is a set, not a character.

**Design gate.** In the bible's `WORLDBUILDING` section, catalog significant places
with: (1) Physical description (sensory), (2) History (what happened), (3)
Meaning per character (what it represents), (4) Arc (how it changes). This is
the place's "character sheet." Use it. The reader should feel the place's weight
every time the character walks through its doors.
